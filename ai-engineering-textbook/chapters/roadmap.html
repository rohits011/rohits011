The 12-Month Learning Roadmap

A progressive, sustainable plan for 10–15 hours per week. It is ordered by leverage for an experienced Java/Spring Boot backend engineer: distributed systems first, then the AI application layer, then the theory beneath it, then the platform around it.

  • Time budget: about 12 hours/week on average: three weekday sessions of 1.5–2 h plus one 4–6 h weekend block.
  • Tracks: a main track (about 9–11 h) and a DSA thread (about 3 h) that runs through months 1–7, then drops to maintenance.
  • Progress: tick assessment criteria below; they are saved in your browser.

How the plan is designed

Prioritisation logic

The order follows three questions: what compounds, what you can apply immediately, and what interviews and promotions actually test.

Reading the diagram: months 1–4 strengthen what you already partly know (backend systems) into senior-level depth, so the return is immediate. Month 5 gives you Python and containers, the two prerequisites for AI work. Months 6–7 put you to work on LLM systems early. Months 8–9 then supply the ML theory, which lands better once you have hands-on context. Months 10–12 round out the platform: Go, delivery pipelines, data, MLOps and infrastructure.

Why LLM engineering comes before ML theory

Opinion Many curricula teach maths → ML → deep learning → LLMs. For an engineer whose goal is to build AI-powered systems, building first and studying theory second is usually more motivating and more marketable. Part 8 recaps every transformer concept it relies on. If you prefer theory first, swap months 6–7 with months 8–9; nothing breaks.

When a week goes badly

Protect the order Must → Should → Could: (1) the chapter's core sections 1–7 and the quiz; (2) the coding assignment; (3) exercises and further reading. Missing the mini project for one month is fine. Missing the fundamentals sections is not, because later months build on them.

Monthly assessment rhythm

Every month ends with the same four checks: (1) each knowledge-check quiz at ≥ 80%; (2) the mini project working end to end, with a README; (3) the month's assessment criteria ticked honestly; (4) a 15-minute explanation of one concept to a colleague, or a written blog-style summary. If you can't explain it, you don't know it yet.

Month 1: Orientation, System Design Foundations & Traffic
